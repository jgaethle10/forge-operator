# NEBRASKA | THE GIANT STIRS
## Evercraft Original Documentary Series | Season One | Director's Bible v2
**Development state:** October 21, 2026. Three ~25-minute episodes, 75-minute target. No complete episode has been filmed, cleared or approved for publication.

### The premise, corrected at the founder's direction

This is **not primarily a documentary about the 2026 Indiana and Oregon games**. Nebraska football itself is the central character: a program with genuine early history, a remarkable institutional rise, five recognized national championships and a football culture that helped define a state; a national power that subsequently experienced fractured leadership, changed football systems and years without championship-level success; and a program now trying to rebuild under Matt Rhule. The 2026 season serves as a contemporary test of the larger historical question rather than the organizing premise of the entire film.

**Series question:** How did Nebraska build a giant, what actually caused it to fall asleep, and what evidence tells us whether it's stirring again?

**Format:** Three 25-minute planning targets. Actual episode runtimes may be 22–28 minutes if the story demands it. There is no justification for filling 75 minutes until the characters, original game footage, sources, legal clearances and observational scenes genuinely support the runtime.

**Investigative standard:** A dramatic causal explanation is not a sourced explanation. We must show primary records, give distinct interview perspectives, distinguish opinions and era-wide trends from provable program decisions, and resist blaming one coach for decades of change. The team's future is not a scripted championship return. A proper finale can conclude that some elements improved while others remain unresolved.

## EPISODE 01 • THE GIANT
**25:00 target | Nebraska's origins through the 1997 championship | Why did this program become a national power?**

The episode should begin with the *height* of Nebraska's achievement, then take viewers backward to the program that made the achievement possible. Nebraska football's five recognized titles are 1970, 1971, 1994, 1995 and 1997. Those five dates are an entry point, not an excuse for 25 minutes of trophy montages. We investigate coaching, field innovation, player development and the state-wide emotional investment that made success repeatable. Original-era interviews and real game footage are required to go beyond a Wikipedia-like chronology.

**00:00–02:20 | The standard.** Open, once properly licensed, on a decisive image from the 1990s dynasty and the real voices of people who remember it. Ask a genuine supporter or historian: what does it mean for one state to believe a program is supposed to win? Do not prewrite the response, license a highlight only to attach generic cheering, or manufacture 1995 announcer audio.

**02:20–06:00 | Before the giant.** Follow Nebraska's early football history from verifiable university and state records. Our actual History Nebraska RG6137.MI motion-picture reel is meaningful because it contains people playing football on an earlier field. The researcher believes part may be circa 1919; some reel material may be even earlier, but exact game years are unproven. Show authentic frames and correct provenance. The original historical recording is treated as *silent*, even if its modern carrier has an unrelated audio stream. Only short portions may be used if History Nebraska's separate written publication-use requirements and possible fees are resolved.

**06:00–10:10 | Devaney builds a contender.** The turning point is Bob Devaney's 1962 arrival and the change from earlier seasons to a program capable of national success. Explore his coaching and organizational decisions. An especially useful primary-source subject is **Boyd Epley's 1969 strength and conditioning program**. Nebraska Athletics documents the program as an innovative collegiate strength-training system. This is a lead for interviews, photographs, training materials and context, not proof that one exercise program independently produced championships.

**10:10–14:50 | Back-to-back, 1970 and 1971.** Tell these seasons through authentic games, original season records, and firsthand perspectives where possible. Nebraska's stated national title records and the 1971 Nebraska-Oklahoma rivalry supply stakes; official results are separate from the copyright rights to broadcast images and calls. The 1973 original National Archives spring-game crowd photograph belongs in the post-title transition, clearly dated as May 1973 and held as a photograph.

**14:50–19:50 | The Osborne formula.** Tom Osborne took over in 1973. What was the actual football system? Examine the walk-on pipeline, strength work, in-state identification, national recruiting, option football, offensive and defensive continuity, the Blackshirts and the changing opposition. Find real staff, former players, independently informed analysts and documentary evidence. Show the work inside the system, not just another succession of scoreboards. Different generations should be free to disagree about which parts mattered most.

**19:50–25:00 | The peak and its inheritance.** Build to the actual 1994, 1995 and 1997 championships with licensed primary-source game action. The end isn't “they became unstoppable forever.” It is the pressure the dynasty created for everyone who inherited it. A nine-win season could come to feel disappointing precisely because of the expectation Nebraska had taught its supporters. Close on an earned question: what happens when the institution that built a dynasty has to function without the people and conditions that first made it possible?

**Episode 1 footage needs:** verified early game film; Devaney/Osborne eras; 1970–71 and 1994–97 licensed game footage; historical staff/player interviews; Epley-era strength records; NARA 1973 real photo; generational supporter testimony; accurate institutional records. No talking archive image can be mistaken for actual moving film.

## EPISODE 02 • THE SLEEPING GIANT
**25:00 target | The post-Osborne transition through 2022 | What went wrong?**

This is the investigative center of the series. A tidy story about “five bad coaches” is not acceptable. Some transitions were turbulent, some eras still won regularly, and years of change in scholarship rules, recruiting, conference competition, player development, university leadership and college football economics require actual evidence. Separate the process of losing national dominance from the later prolonged stretches of outright losing.

**00:00–02:50 | A crack in the certainty.** The 2001 loss to Colorado, 62–36, is an important visual and emotional pivot, if footage can be properly licensed. But one score cannot explain two decades. Ask what underlying competitive and institutional changes were already occurring by that date.

**02:50–07:00 | Solich and the decision.** After Osborne, Frank Solich went 58–19 across six seasons. The team finished 7–7 in 2002, yet Solich was dismissed after a 9–3 2003 regular season. Examine contemporaneous results, administration statements and reasonable competing accounts of the decision. Don't invent the motivation or claim everyone considered his run a failure.

**07:00–11:00 | A different formula.** Bill Callahan introduced the West Coast Offense, and Nebraska experienced losing seasons in 2004 and 2007. Show real period footage, what systems changed and how the program's recruiting, defensive performance and development adjusted. Evaluate results and choices without retroactively making individual coaches mouth invented explanations.

**11:00–15:20 | The Pelini paradox.** Bo Pelini delivered *at least nine wins in each of seven seasons* from 2008–14, and Nebraska still failed to win a conference title. The 2011 transition to the Big Ten occurred during this period. Ask why the institution and portions of the fan base viewed the ceiling differently and what was actually changing. The fact that a nine-win coach was dismissed must not be edited as if the program had a losing record in those years.

**15:20–21:00 | The long darkness.** Treat the Mike Riley and Scott Frost periods as distinct eras with different staff, circumstances, decisions, football evidence and documented results. Explain how Nebraska moved from regular bowl expectations to the postseason absence that ended in 2024. Interviewing real players and fans about this period is central, but do not stage grief, manufacture blame or imply any subject's endorsement.

**21:00–25:00 | The autopsy, not the verdict.** Compare possible contributors: athletic department leadership changes, consistent or inconsistent football identity, development and strength practices, scholarship and recruiting shifts, competition in the Big Ten, national college football's changing landscape, and NIL/transfer systems where chronologically relevant. Mark clearly which are demonstrable facts, which are argued causes, and what evidence is missing. End with Nebraska's conditions when Matt Rhule arrived, not with an unsupported declaration that everything had been fixed or hopelessly broken.

**Episode 2 footage needs:** actual 2001 Colorado game, 2002–03 university records, era-specific Callahan/Pelini/Riley/Frost video and source voices, Big Ten conference history, independent football/program historian, and supporters who experienced these changes. Historical contemporaneous reports and interviews outrank retrospective slogans.

## EPISODE 03 • THE GIANT STIRS
**25:00 target | 2023–2026 and the future | Is the program actually being rebuilt?**

This is where the current Nebraska season belongs. Investigate the work Matt Rhule and his staff say they are doing, then compare it with independent evidence, actual game performances, player development, organizational continuity and fan expectations. The film should be able to recognize genuine progress and frustrating shortcomings simultaneously.

**00:00–03:35 | What Rhule inherited.** Show the documented football baseline when Matt Rhule took charge for 2023. The Huskers went 5–7 in 2023. Identify program constraints from records, the staff's public plans and legitimate independent sources. Do not borrow a coach's public-relations description as the documentary's unchallenged narration.

**03:35–08:15 | Rebuilding a system.** Examine present-day recruiting, roster age/retention, coaching and position development, training and facilities, the transfer portal and NIL/revenue-sharing economics with real practice footage or observable operations *where access is approved*. In the original dynasty, Nebraska built an institutional competitive advantage. Which contemporary program practices actually resemble that, and which are fundamentally different in 2026? This echo between Episodes 1 and 3 is the thematic payoff.

**08:15–12:25 | Milestones, not declarations.** Nebraska went 7–6 in 2024 and defeated Boston College 20–15 in the Pinstripe Bowl, the program's first bowl victory since 2015 and its first winning season since 2016. The Huskers finished 7–6 again in 2025. These are measurable improvements over some earlier years but do not prove a return to national contention. Give each year its real context and original game footage only when licensed.

**12:25–17:25 | Five wins, then ten seconds.** Nebraska began 2026 at 5–0. The Indiana 20–17 loss and Oregon 17–14 loss are the sharp modern test. Our source-locked *Ten Seconds* chapter can become a **2:18 candidate component here**, not an entire episode or series premise. Nebraska's defense registered 11 tackles for loss and held Oregon to 242 yards, yet Oregon won on a late touchdown. Preserve both the evidence of competitiveness and what remained unresolved. A broadcast account and original game soundtrack require clearance; a timeline based on official game results is not itself broadcast footage.

**17:25–21:55 | Unfinished games.** Nebraska-Washington on October 31, 2026 and subsequent November opponents are future outcomes as of this bible's date. Reconfirm the official schedule and capture what actually happens. Do not write scripted victories or a predetermined spiral. Real participant follow-ups can show changing expectations across the season.

**21:55–25:00 | Where the giant goes.** The ending must be earned by the eventual record, program evidence and people we genuinely filmed. Ask whether Nebraska is improving, whether its leadership and infrastructure are durable, and which steps remain before national relevance. A stronger third episode could end with hope, doubt, or an unresolved question. It may not end with an invented national-title promise.

**Episode 3 footage needs:** genuine Rhule-era 2023–25 material, real training/program sources, licensed 2024 bowl and 2026 game action, factual roster and team development evidence, interviews with both generations of supporters and independent analysts, and actual remaining-season results after they occur.

## The connective creative grammar

There are three mirrors across the trilogy. **Original early Nebraska players vs contemporary players**, not a fabricated comparison of identical plays. **A championship-development institution vs a program that lost continuity**, assessed with real evidence. **The expectation inherited by generations of fans vs the performance the current players can actually achieve.** Real archived motion, true original sound, dated stills, filmed human interviews and judicious original scoring are the tools. A dramatic logo, artificial crowd bed, synthetic former-coach voice or pulsing montage is not a substitute for a coherent explanation.

A useful recurring human trio: a longtime supporter, a younger supporter who did not experience the 1990s dynasty, and an independent historian/reporter knowledgeable about Nebraska football. Program participants, assistant coaches or former players can add unique first-person accounts if access and appropriate releases exist. Do not assume anyone has agreed. Offer room for perspectives that challenge the premise that this is already a “turnaround.” Keep the actual voices, context and affiliations intact.

### Historical and legal standards

The evidence file `history.evidence.json` records what can be sourced and which causes remain investigative. `series.sources.json` separately describes media, people, archival bytes and rights. `source-rights-docket.json` tracks archive licensors. Factual news links are not the rights to show original highlights. The authenticated History Nebraska moving reel is believed free of known restrictions under U.S. copyright but still faces History Nebraska's separate *written publication permission* requirement, possible use fees and international copyright review. The 1973 crowd still is an original NARA DOCUMERICA federal photograph. The 2011 Lincoln sky and 2026 Kregel workshop footage are real optional environmental B-roll, not default padding. Both Commons licenses need their actual attribution/adaptation terms reviewed. No 2026 game broadcast footage or interviews have yet been licensed or captured. The related four archive, rights and press Gmail inquiries remain UNSENT.

**Existing genuine Evercraft work:** The native *Before the Echo RC08* is an authenticated 50-second private research-viewing film candidate. The *Ten Seconds* chapter is a 138-second sourced editorial PLAN requiring actual Indiana/Oregon video and participant recording. Neither constitutes an approved standalone public scene. The first belongs in Episode 1 research; the latter belongs in Episode 3.

**Production priority:** Source interviews and licensed original archival and championship footage for Episodes 1 and 2 *in parallel* with documenting the rest of 2026 for Episode 3. Produce a compelling 4–6-minute Episode 1 sequence, preferably with an authentic person, actual historically relevant footage and a concrete question. Ask a viewer unfamiliar with Nebraska whom they cared about and what they now wonder. Solve quality before expanding to 25 minutes. The film studio builds proof-carrying editable projects; it does not grant source rights or director approval.

### Evidence and gate acceptance

Six editorial sequences each across three episodes target 4,500 seconds. Actual audience-worthy footage minutes are NOT inferred from planned durations. The automated `series-production-gate.mjs` validates the three correct episode arcs, 20 attributed research claims, all 33 registered current source/participant requirements, absence of future game results, no false archive licensing, and no automatic publication. Native film QA covers visual motion, still-image integrity, source checksums and codec checks. Only a human director can decide whether a scene is dramatic, credible and respectful enough to include. Permission to contact third parties, spend, merge or publish must be deliberate and separately authorized.

**Truth:** This is a documentary about a program that historically won five national titles, lost its national preeminence, and is now trying to recover it. It is *not* a documentary announcing that the recovery is complete.
