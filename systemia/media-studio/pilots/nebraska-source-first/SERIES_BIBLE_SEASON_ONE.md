# NEBRASKA | THE GIANT STIRS
## Evercraft Original Documentary | Three-part Season One | Development Bible v1

**Working format:** Three documentary episodes targeting 25 minutes each, with a permissible creative range of 22–28 minutes per episode. Total planning budget is 75 minutes; this is NOT an assertion that 75 minutes of usable film exist.

**As-of:** October 21, 2026. Nebraska is 5–2 following five wins, a 17–20 defeat against Indiana (October 10), and a 14–17 defeat at Oregon (October 17). The next scheduled game is Washington at Nebraska on October 31. All subsequent outcomes, rights and recorded interviews remain unknown. Source: https://bigten.org/schedule.aspx?schedule=8494

**Series logline:** A century of football lives in Nebraska's fields, old reels, stadiums and families. In 2026, a talented team offers its supporters reasons to believe, then gives them two fourth quarters they cannot forget. Across three episodes, real people confront what Nebraska football has meant, what it actually is now, and what it might still become.

**For whom:** A viewer who has never watched a Cornhuskers game should finish episode one caring about at least two specific real people. No assumed knowledge of Big Ten standings, coaches or campus geography. A Husker fan should find the archival record truthful and the 2026 analysis fair enough to disagree with intelligently, not an advertisement for a comeback that has not happened.

### EPISODE 1 | The Inheritance | target 25:00

**Central question:** Why do people inherit a football team that cannot promise them anything?

**00:00–01:45 | A human hook:** Start with something observed, not a map of championships. The original historic players and one currently living supporter are linked by an honestly recorded memory, if and only if a real consenting supporter says something worth cutting to. Never write their answer in advance. A cold open is not guaranteed until we have the actual voice.

**01:45–05:30 | A state beyond football:** Lincoln air, campus and stadium life, how ordinary places become ritual. The 2011 real moving cloud/windmill recording is optional geographic atmosphere, clearly dated. The 2026 Kregel workshop film is also optional and belongs only if a real human's Nebraska working-life story motivates it. Neither is footage of contemporary Husker gameplay.

**05:30–10:00 | The earliest surviving players:** The verified History Nebraska RG6137.MI source reel, probably including circa-1919 game action and an early forward pass, carries genuine moving human activity. The exact year is unresolved. Source film is known to be silent for historical-capture purposes; its digitized audio track is not claimed as 1919 field noise. We do not force 4.5 minutes of this same reel; we acquire a real historian/researcher or other primary evidence and allow the sequence to contract.

**10:00–14:45 | A sea of red through time:** The authentic May 1973 crowd still, NARA 547323 by Charles O'Rear, is a visual anchor, not a five-minute moving photograph. Pursue cleared historical moving game or audience footage and dated eyewitness recollections. Explain meaningful football era changes with evidence rather than reciting a trophy list.

**14:45–20:50 | The inheritance has faces:** Give a long-time fan and a younger fan room to differ, remember and revise. Film their real environment and direct experience, not prewritten reactions. Their genuine voices and choices should determine whether this is the emotional center.

**20:50–25:00 | The burden of the present:** Introduce the genuine 2026 team and the question of finishing games using rights-cleared real game imagery or sourced factual reporting. Neither future success nor an inevitable collapse can be asserted. An act-ending question is earned by what the people actually say.

**Creative hurdle:** Can a neutral viewer name or remember a real person afterward? If not, this episode is not ready for 25 minutes.

### EPISODE 2 | Ten Seconds | target 25:00

**Central question:** A team won five games to open 2026. How much can the last minutes reveal about who it really is?

**00:00–01:30 | Ten seconds:** Nebraska went up 17–6 over Indiana at Q4 12:01 on October 10. At Q4 11:51, Indiana's 75-yard touchdown made it 17–13. Tell the genuine visual sequence only if the relevant actual clips and original broadcast sound are licensed. Otherwise a verified, plainly labeled timeline can be researched, but not masqueraded as the play.

**01:30–05:50 | Five wins, real context:** Establish the actual 5–0 opening and opponents from official results, including what observers felt was improving and what evidence limited that optimism. Avoid presenting a schedule montage without people or stakes.

**05:50–12:20 | Indiana, as witnessed:** Show the decisive real play sequence, scoreboard time, sidelines and crowd reaction where sourced and cleared. Indiana took the 20–17 lead on a 44-yard fourth-down reception with 7:13 left. Interview honest witnesses and analysts separately, and do not manufacture reactions or game announcer quotes.

**12:20–19:10 | Oregon, a different mechanism:** Nebraska recorded 11 tackles for loss and held Oregon to 242 yards, led 14–6 entering the fourth and lost 17–14 after a final touchdown at 0:28. Establish why the performance and the result can coexist, without declaring it “the same collapse.” Exact 2026 licensed visual events required.

**19:10–22:40 | The people after two losses:** Return to the Episode 1 people and let them disagree about the evidence, patience and expectations. If no consented interviews exist, the act is not recorded.

**22:40–25:00 | No false resolution:** At this as-of date, the Washington game on October 31 has not been played. End with an open question rather than a prepackaged triumphant swell.

**Creative hurdle:** The episode must show meaningful insight beyond a sports recap, with at least one recorded voice changing how a factual play is understood.

### EPISODE 3 | The Giant Stirs | target 25:00, ending deliberately unwritten

**Central question:** What will this season ultimately mean to people who lived it?

**00:00–02:00 | Return to the unresolved:** The previous episode's actual unanswered question. No voiceover suggesting knowledge of future results.

**02:00–07:00 | Washington as it happens:** Nebraska vs Washington, scheduled October 31, is a *future source requirement*. Verify the official result, film access and context after the event.

**07:00–13:50 | The remaining regular season:** Follow November football and changes in expectations. Do not build prewritten wins, losses, coach quotes, fan celebrations or injuries. All dates and opponent details should be reconfirmed against official schedules before writing captions.

**13:50–18:40 | Beyond the record:** An independent credible analyst or journalist and real filmed participants examine actual results, not a projected win total. What did the team prove? What remained unproven?

**18:40–23:05 | Return to the people:** Let the same supporters revisit their hopes. The film should be capable of showing disappointment, complexity, joy, or all three without forcing a redemption arc.

**23:05–25:00 | An earned last image:** The ending is not predetermined. Choose from original live documentary material only after the season's genuine outcomes and filmed voices provide meaning. The title *The Giant Stirs* is not a claim that a championship revival already occurred.

**Creative hurdle:** An ending that works even if the team loses more games. The audience should believe the film looked for truth rather than a predetermined feel-good result.

### Filmmaking grammar

Each sequence needs a subject doing or experiencing something, a reason to observe it, and a reason to cut. Leave authentic pauses, location microphones and expressive human voices intact. Do not pad runtime with artificial camera motion over photographs, fake retro broadcast noise, slow-motion reenactments presented as facts, endless talking heads or atmospheric stock footage. Modest original score can be composed after an edit has a rhythm, credited as original music, not disguised as historic scene sound. Don't mistake loudness or expensive graphics for emotion. Historical label design must be small enough to respect faces and legible enough to communicate date uncertainty.

**Core recurring cast to recruit, not fabricated:** One older/long-time supporter, one younger supporter with a different perspective, and one independent researcher or football analyst. The older and younger participants should ideally appear in all three episodes. Do not select subjects only because they will deliver the preferred “giant is back” line. Film both their personal history and responses to real evolving events. Consent, recording identity, release terms and any conflict of interest must be preserved.

### A practical sequence of operations

**First, acquire the right to tell this story.** Reconcile any new incoming replies before contacting the same party. Review the already prepared archive/broadcast access inquiries and send only when approved. Source every existing original archive with attribution and sha/duration receipts.

**Second, record real humans.** Identify two independent generations of fans and one credible contextual voice, capture an honest 30–60-minute conversation with each where possible, and preserve releases, raw media, verbatim transcript, scene location and timestamps. Do not assume availability, impersonate or invent answers.

**Third, cut a genuinely compelling five-minute Episode 1 sequence.** Use a real person and a real archived moment with a motivated transition. The existing 50-second scene becomes a *candidate scene*, not an automatic five-minute filler. Have someone unfamiliar with Nebraska watch and explain who they cared about. If they can't, keep recutting or capture stronger material before assembling 25 minutes.

**Fourth, develop Episodes 1 and 2 concurrently.** Episode 2 requires the exact Indiana and Oregon plays and original broadcast audio rights. If not cleared, the story must be restructured honestly around real interviews and independent licensed material rather than simulated plays.

**Fifth, observe the rest of 2026 without scripting it.** Episode 3 story locks only after the actual games and characters' responses are captured. Build a 60–90-second trailer only from cleared shots, after there is a genuine story in the edit.

**Deliverables and checks:** Raw masters preserved, each source hash verified, labeled footage dates, signed rights and releases separately checked, source-recorded sound continuously tested, shots individually reviewed for observed action, a human review of caption legibility, full 1080p master decode, dialogue/sound mix listening, founder creative approval and separate explicit publishing authorization. CI proves structural/technical contracts, not the greatness or commercial readiness of the documentary.
