## What the founder caught in the old orchestration

The first VSCO score was not a real piano-led counterpoint, and its timbre was
not yet convincing. Examples from the actual original files:

- At 13.55 seconds, cello **F3** sustained while piano **B4** was sounding:
  an exposed tritone, with the F natural foreign to the song's established
  E-natural-minor/F-sharp vocabulary.
- At 14.73 seconds, violin **B4** continued while piano sounded **A4**:
  a held major second without an intentional or earned resolution.
- More simultaneous note-writing did not create an emotional response to
  the piano melody. A technically source-verified sampler is not a human
  string player.

Dissonance is not globally forbidden. The point is *intentional voice
leading and melodic relevance*, not machine-composed always-consonant pads.

## The *separate* instrument realism admission gate

Passing harmonics != passing timbre. These are independent decisions:

1. **Isolated note/tone:** Compare quiet and strong native cello and violin
   takes. Preserve the native acoustic attack, string scrape and natural
   recorded vibrato. Do not erase bows by 200ms attack ramps or invent a
   12% sine-wave-shaped "phrase" by applying generic gain modulation.
   Keep minimal click prevention only.
2. **Held-note quality:** Acoustic sample must have genuine sustain for the
   score duration, no obvious baked-in loop, tonal buzzing, duplicated
   reverb, or abrupt cutoff. If it fails, **reject the recording**, not
   just hide the problem with effects.
3. **Transitions:** Even individual prerecorded note layers are NOT
   authentic played legato. To pass orchestral realism, capture or license
   at least the real **E3→G3→E3**, **C3→G3→E3** cello gestures, and
   **E4→G4**, **G4→E4** violin gestures as performed phrases, with slurred
   and separated-bow variants, intentional vibrato, bow reversal,
   releases and dynamic arc. Save room/close microphones, take and player
   attribution, permission, hashes and alignment markers.
4. **Phrase playback:** Implement an explicit transition/phrase asset lane
   with a verified sample resolver and source-appropriate overlap;
   never pretend concatenated note WAVs are original performed legato.
   Fail closed when a requested gesture lacks recorded coverage.
5. **Dynamics:** The two VSCO soft/strong source layers are a *pilot*, not
   sufficient for world-class continuous dynamics. For every instrument,
   build several actual recorded velocity/dynamic zones and round robins;
   never relabel an attenuated loud sample as genuine pianissimo.
6. **Piano too:** Preserve founder-approved V1 exactly. Develop an opt-in
   sibling Steinway instrument with full pedal-up/down, damper-off,
   sympathetic and string resonance, room/close sources, releases and
   repeated-note alternatives; compare blind against original. A green
   waveform QC test cannot approve a piano tone.
7. **Orchestra expansion:** Violin and cello must pass phrase auditions
   *independently* before viola, bass, winds, brass, percussion or
   additional sections are layered in. Multi-seat writing must be
   musician-led, with real playable breathing and bow changes.

## Required listening acceptance (human, not automated)

Produce piano-only reference alongside isolated cello (soft/strong),
isolated violin (soft/strong), true phrase gestures (when captured),
piano+cello and piano+cello+violin. Keep edits at matched listening level;
also listen at normal unnormalized volume. Ask the founder:
- "Does the cello **answer** the piano?"
- "Do the violin notes feel earned, or are they an unrelated tune?"
- "Where can you hear a sampler instead of someone bowing?"
- "Would you rather hear nothing but the piano at this moment?"

An answer of "terrible" or "doesn't fit" is **rejected**, regardless
of source license, CI status, loudness, or note-in-key checks.
