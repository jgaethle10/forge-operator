# REIGNITE | HANGAR B
### The original festival vision | preservation-first feasibility concept

**For discussion with Vintage Aviation Museum and the Port of Tillamook Bay. This is a concept, not an authorized or announced event.**

Hangar B is a one-of-a-kind part of Northwest aviation history, and it has been part of generations of family memories. REIGNITE is our idea for a spectacular, international-facing music, art, food and heritage gathering that draws people into its story and generates a transparent, agreed contribution toward restoration. Its production has to protect the very building it celebrates.

## The festival we actually designed

**1. The outdoor main stage.** Our primary large-performance space: electronic music and other international, national and regional artists, designed around a separately approved outdoor location, professional sound and production, lawful operating hours and a sound-propagation plan that protects Hangar B, neighboring uses and airport operations. It is not a fallback. Stage construction, crowd loading, utilities and emergency access require independent specialists and approval.

**2. The indoor visual/acoustic dome.** The intimate counterpoint **inside Hangar B, if the museum and qualified reviews permit it**: a freestanding, reversible visual dome with extraordinary projection, immersive light and live acoustic or otherwise demonstrably low-impact intimate sets. This is a full program space, **not merely a few visuals and not a subwoofer-driven dance floor**. It cannot attach to historic timber, displace exhibits, block exits, or be represented as soundproof before engineering and acoustical evidence.

**3. The Quiet Wing.** A separate calm area with comfortable seating, headphones and soft visuals where guests can decompress away from the main-stage energy. This is distinct from the dome, with accessibility, sound isolation and life-safety requirements of its own.

**4. Participate, don't just watch.** Safe hosted guest-DJ opportunities and karaoke; art installations and exhibitions; an aviation/memory-themed photography contest; licensed food vendors and possible cook-offs; and interactive heritage storytelling.

**5. Give back and go global.** A museum-centered heritage exhibit, a possible silent auction and sponsorship/fundraising program with clearly agreed beneficiary and reporting rules. Invite artistic interest from around the world once event authority and any performer-specific U.S. work authorization, contracts and fees are handled correctly.

**6. Optional aviation gathering.** A community pilot suggested a possible coordinated fly-in. This is a future feasibility question for the airport operator/Port and museum, not a scheduled aircraft event, arrival instruction, parking reservation or proposed airshow.

**Experience arc:** welcoming daytime heritage, food and exhibitions; intimate dome, Quiet Wing and community activities through the day/evening; and the outdoor main stage as the primary high-energy nighttime experience **within whatever hours and restrictions are actually authorized**. No all-night schedule is assumed.

## Preliminary scope to test, not a cap on the vision

Summer 2027, potentially July/August, is an exploratory window. A **300–500-person first operating scenario** is a preliminary model for museum discussion, **not a final festival target, certified occupancy or a decision to shrink the concept**. Approved capacity, length, dates, parking, site geography, heritage protections and operating hours have not been established.

## Designed around three clear review boundaries

- **Museum/hangar:** Vintage Aviation Museum's written venue permission, current roof/structural repair restrictions, museum exhibits, approved visitor zones, non-invasive temporary installations, occupancy, fire and accessible routes.
- **Outdoor event and airport:** the Port's underlying property/airport authority and written decisions on outdoor site use, ground access, sound, curfew, parking, airport interface and any optional fly-in. A stage location cannot be inferred from our concept map.
- **Professional delivery and preservation funding:** qualified event production, structural/acoustical/fire/medical/security/insurance/permit leads; a separate written museum benefit agreement before claiming charitable status or collecting funds. Evercraft supplies concept, creative direction, sponsor materials, communications and simple participant coordination.

## The decision we are asking for now

Not permission to sell tickets or book a stage. We would value **one preliminary site-feasibility conversation** identifying the right museum/Port contacts, any areas worth evaluating for an outdoor stage and a non-invasive indoor dome, current exclusions and repair/engineering documents, and whether the new Hangar B Preservation Council is the best route for alignment. If the dome, amplified outdoor music or larger attendance cannot be made safe, we adapt the configuration with reviewers rather than risk the hangar.

**The ambition stays intact. The approvals determine what can be built, where, and when.**

*Independent Evercraft planning concept. Vintage Aviation Museum acquired Hangar B and reopened Tillamook Air Museum in October 2026; the Port retained the underlying land. Neither organization has authorized REIGNITE, ticketing, fundraising or airport operations.*
