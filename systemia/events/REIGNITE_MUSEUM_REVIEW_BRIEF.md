# HANGAR B: REIGNITE
## A preservation-centered gathering at Tillamook Air Museum
**Concept for discussion with Vintage Aviation Museum | 25 November 2026**

### Why this exists
Hangar B isn't just an extraordinary piece of timber engineering. It's where generations of families encountered aviation history together. REIGNITE imagines a way to bring new people into that story and create a meaningful, transparent contribution toward its preservation. The hangar is the reason to gather, and its protection comes before any spectacle.

### What we're proposing, not announcing

The **primary creative concept** is a multi-zone preservation festival with a **professionally produced outdoor main stage** and a **separate indoor freestanding acoustic/visual dome**, not a small quiet exhibit with an outdoor performance as an afterthought. The outdoor stage carries high-energy electronic and cross-genre music, including potential international performers. The dome inside Hangar B, **only if authorized**, hosts intimate acoustic or demonstrably low-impact sets under extraordinary projection and immersive visuals. The **Quiet Wing is a third, separate space**, with headphones, comfortable seats and a sensory reset. Together these contrasting experiences are the heart of the design.

The full program also includes hosted guest-DJ opportunities, karaoke, art exhibitions, a photography competition, licensed food vendors and potential cooking contests, Hangar B historical storytelling, a silent auction, sponsors and an appropriately agreed preservation-benefit arrangement. A community-proposed aviation fly-in is **additional and optional**, requiring separate Port/airport authority.

Our desired arc is daytime history, art and community activities; intimate dome and Quiet Wing experiences; and a major **outdoor** evening stage within verified operating hours. The dome must be freestanding and reversible, require no contact with the historic fabric, and must be assessed for structural, acoustic, fire, egress, exhibit and accessibility impacts. Its immersive visuals cannot be misdescribed as soundproofing or permission for subwoofers. The stage requires its own approved location, sound review, weather engineering, crowd control, utilities and airport/neighbor assessment. No zone is approved now.

**Feasibility options are safety responses, not replacements for our creative brief:** (A) full outdoor-stage + indoor-dome multi-zone concept when all permissions and engineering allow; (B) outdoor stage with an alternate museum-approved low-impact visual area if the dome is not permissible; (C) small preservation-first showcase if concert production cannot be safely authorized. We would not make a risky event happen just to preserve a layout drawing.

Summer 2027 (possibly July or August) is an exploratory date window. **300–500 participants is only an initial pilot planning scenario, not the vision's maximum audience or authorized occupancy.** Final festival scale, duration and capacities require the museum, Port/airport, accessibility/fire/permit authorities, insurance, licensed production and applicable legal sound hours.

### A community idea worth exploring, separately
A visiting pilot suggested inviting the regional aviation community to participate, perhaps through a **small coordinated fly-in**. It is an exciting *optional* possibility, not part of the baseline attendance or venue assumptions. Before mentioning arrivals or parking publicly, Vintage and the **Port/airport operator** would need to identify the correct scope of airport approvals, safe airside separation, parking capacity, weather contingencies and whether the concept is a normal fly-in or another type of aviation event. There are **no flight registrations, reserved aircraft spaces or airshow plans**.

### What Evercraft would take responsibility for
Creative concept and story; accessible online communications; a distinctive but restrained public identity; potential artist/sponsor research and tailored presentation materials; and a clear participant experience coordinated through an owned planning system. A **qualified Oregon live-event production partner**, plus the appropriate structural, acoustical, fire, airport and event professionals, would lead all technical event design, engineering, permitting, operations and on-site safety. Evercraft is not representing those external professionals or claiming completed engineering work.

### What we need from the museum first
**One short feasibility review**, not an event commitment:
- Who can speak for the **current Hangar B owner/operator** on preliminary venue use? Vintage Aviation Museum confirms its acquisition, but we understand that the Port retains the underlying land and operates the airport.
- Are there any **areas or types of visitor activity** worth evaluating given the current roof repairs, exclusion zones, historic fabric, exhibit operations and emergency routes? We will not guess an approved footprint from a conceptual map.
- Is there an existing **structural condition / repair / site-use reference** the museum can share or identify for qualified review, including the appropriate engineering or preservation contact?
- Would it be more helpful to begin with the **Hangar B Preservation Council** or a designated museum liaison, and what realistic summer 2027 timing should we use for discussion?

### Preservation and financial integrity
Any charitable component would require a written agreement covering the beneficiary, permitted representations, sponsor obligations, event costs, net proceeds, custody of funds and reporting. We would not collect donations, sell tickets, announce a sanctioned event, or promise a restoration amount without the proper agreements.

**The immediate ask:** permission to refine one modest, sound-conscious feasibility scenario with the right museum contact and site constraints. A safe decision to say "not here" or "not yet" is a successful preservation decision too.
