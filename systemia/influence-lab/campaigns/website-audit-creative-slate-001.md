# Influence Lab creative slate 001 | Website Audit

**State:** Concept/review only. Source: #1389, PR #1390; pricing, live route, checkout, fulfillment, original media and release permission require independent re-verification before publication. This document is not a published campaign and does not imply customer outcomes.

## Creative strategy

**Buyer:** operator of a small business whose website feels fine but may be silently frustrating visitors. **Truthful promise:** a bounded review of publicly visible web experiences can find specific things worth fixing. Do not claim a given business is losing revenue without relevant evidence. For the existing $49 Quick Win Audit, verify current price, exact five-fix scope and checkout/fulfillment before putting dollar claims in copy.

**One-line idea:** “Your best work deserves a way through.”

No claims of increased sales, conversion uplift or guaranteed customers. Point to a live demonstrable audit and verifiable scoped next step.

## Evercraft proposed design codes v0.1 (not a global brand change)

- **Shape:** one human-centered focal object against deep open space, not ornamental dashboards. Letterforms should feel engineered but approachable; motion creates the signature more than a decorative logo.
- **Light:** electric refractive glass used *sparingly* to expose cause and effect, with physical texture such as paper, skin, workbench or device edges for humanity. No 1990s perspective grids, faux holograms or off-brand copies of named fashion houses.
- **Energy:** each transition should tell a story: blocked → noticed → understood → one clear action. Glow is an accent, not evidence.
- **Rhythm:** proposed original 88-BPM 4/4 cue with a four-part, eight-bar arc (~21.8s). Cut on meaningful story turns, never mechanically flash every beat. Maintain captions and sound-off comprehension.
- **Color:** strong neutral contrast with one luminous accent per product; validate against existing Evercraft brand settings before production.
- **Photography:** real people and real software for observed claims. Concept footage visibly labeled. No AI-generated “happy customer” presented as genuine.
- **Accessibility:** safe contrast, readable mobile text, captions, non-flashing motion, descriptive alt text.

## Treatment A: THE DESIGNER / “The Passage” (21.8s)

Editorial intent: make a normally invisible problem visible without pretending to know the buyer's current website metrics.

| Time | Image / camera | Sound / copy |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–5.5s | Real hand unlocks phone. A clean, inviting glass plane refracts one tap path. | One soft tactile click; low original motif. On-screen: “A website can look finished.” |
| 5.5–10.9s | Interface path becomes opaque where friction *could* occur. Clearly label illustration as a concept. | Low-frequency tension. On-screen: “That doesn't mean every path feels easy.” |
| 10.9–16.4s | Hard cut to real, permissioned audit evidence showing a bounded, explainable check. No fabricated defects. | Rhythm resolves; narrator: “Look at the experience from the visitor's side.” |
| 16.4–21.8s | Actual verified landing page + one legible CTA. No false checkout animation. | Original 4-note motif. On-screen: “Understand the friction. Fix what matters.” |

**Release prerequisites:** usable actual app footage, owned/licensed score, mobile legibility, product screenshot proof, exact working CTA.

## Treatment B: THE HUMAN / “Show Your Work” (25–40s)

Unscripted first-person walkthrough. No invented testimonial or fake customer. Ask a real operator to show their process:
1. What are you looking for when visiting a small-business site for the first time?
2. What is one *demonstrated* issue you can explain in ordinary language?
3. What would the buyer receive, and what would **not** be included?
4. Can you show the actual workflow, including any limitation, without cutting around the inconvenient bits?

Visuals are unpolished but not sloppy: camera, readable screen capture, authentic ambient sound, honest time stamps where relevant. If founder or team member speaks, disclose that relationship. Finish with one truthful URL and scoped next step. This film cannot be generated from imaginary customer dialogue.

## Treatment C: THE PROOF / “The Click That Matters” (20–30s)

A small-business owner asks a natural question: “Why does my site get visitors but not enough inquiries?” Present this as an illustrative question, **not** a claimed diagnosis of an actual client's site. Show the verified website audit workflow: input URL, observed output and source-labeled five-fix result. Highlight a specific real limitation (e.g., public-view audit cannot prove private checkout analytics). Finish with the current verified offer scope and price after canary checks.

**Proof standard:** complete capture of one real authorized demo; real timestamps, source URLs, no fake traffic, no invented customer, and no implied paid receipt where none exists.
