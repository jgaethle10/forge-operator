## Cross-check outcome

1. **Supported:** linked serrated saws were first developed in Scottish medical practice in the 1780s, with Aitken's obstetric context and Jeffray's orthopedic context differing.
2. **Supported:** Aitken's instrument was illustrated in 1785; evidence of use in a dissecting room is explicit in the 2004 scholarly abstract.
3. **Supported:** Heine developed the hand-cranked guided-chain osteotome in 1830. Museums retain later surviving examples.
4. **Supported with caution:** symphysiotomy was risky. Do not imply a chain saw made the procedure safe or that obstetric patients routinely experienced chainsaw use.
5. **Not established:** a specific straight-through manufacturing lineage from the 1780s surgical chain to any branded modern forestry machine.
6. **Rights:** original newsroom SVG is self-authored and not dependent on third-party photos. It is an explanatory **concept**, not a literal technical reconstruction. External museum photos are not cleared.
