# Evercraft Journal | Curiosity Desk & Gravity Distribution Standard
**Effective:** 2026-10-31
**Authority:** Founder editorial direction; supplements, does not weaken, the existing Journal evidence/rights/editorial gate, Systemia approval, and Evercraft Clip publication authority.
**Purpose:** Give people a reason to follow Evercraft even when they are not looking to buy a product.

## Editorial identity

Evercraft Journal should be a destination for fascinating, verifiable things happening in the real world. Surprise opens the door; an honest explanation earns the reader's trust. Products and services may be relevant to some stories, but are not the default subject or the hidden pitch.

A reader should finish each feature with three things:
1. An actual discovery, event, mystery or engineering achievement worth knowing.
2. A clear separation between what was measured, what experts infer and what is still uncertain.
3. A question, implication or next development worth returning to the Journal for.

## Desks to scout in parallel

- **Science & Discovery:** space, biology, geology, physics, oceans, unusual experiments and scientific revisions.
- **Hidden History:** Cold War architecture, buried infrastructure, lost or transformed places, documented historical puzzles.
- **Engineering & Invention:** robotics, energy, materials, aerospace, infrastructure and real-world prototypes.
- **The Future in Motion:** consequential technology deployments, computer science and AI developments; claims tied to actual demonstrations or documentation.
- **Where We Live:** regional discoveries and larger phenomena with real local impact, especially the Pacific Northwest.

Do not serialize all editorial work through a single product or desk. A new substantive article can run independently of commercial launches.

## Selection rule: reader gravity, not noise

Rank every candidate on 0-5 each:
- **Astonishment:** Is the verified finding genuinely surprising?
- **Consequence:** Why could this matter to real people, technology or future research?
- **Evidence:** Is there an accessible primary source and credible corroboration?
- **Explanation:** Can Evercraft add understandable context beyond the original headline?
- **Visual opportunity:** Is a rights-cleared factual visual or original data visualization possible?
- **Return potential:** Is there a responsible follow-up, open question or update to track?

A candidate must have source credibility, not just a high score. Novelty alone never justifies publishing a rumor. Prefer original papers, agencies, scientific institutions, official archives and documented field evidence. Distinguish independent corroboration from multiple pages repeating the same press release. Avoid making the Journal a copied headline feed.

## Standard story arc

**Hook:** One clear, true sentence that earns attention without exaggeration.

**What happened:** Names, place, observation date, publication date, primary evidence and what instruments or records show.

**How it works:** A plain-language explanation, with the depth that makes this worth reading rather than merely resharing.

**Why it matters:** Credible consequences, practical stakes and engineering relevance where supported.

**What it does not mean:** The most likely misleading reading of the finding, corrected without scolding the reader.

**What comes next:** Unresolved questions, upcoming measurements or the evidence that would change the current picture.

Stories should be substantive, mobile-readable, original and multi-paragraph. Maintain the existing editorial quality standard. No decorative text cards as substitutes for documentary evidence or meaningful imagery.

## Required editorial packet

Every candidate should include:
- working title, desk, source event date and story preparation date;
- primary source, secondary/independent corroboration where possible, citation URLs;
- claim ledger separating observed, reported, modeled and inferred facts;
- uncertainty and competing explanations;
- rights and provenance of every photograph, chart, video, audio track and quotation;
- substantive original article;
- platform-specific social drafts, all marked **HOLD** until a valid release;
- Fallen visual/motion companion brief with accurate scientific/historical labels;
- defined recheck conditions for later corrections;
- publication and distribution receipt ledger.

A Journal story is not approved just because a model has written a polished draft. The existing 10/10 editorial, sourcing, freshness, Fallen, source-visual-rights and Systemia-release requirements remain binding. Neither the pipeline nor an operator should manufacture missing gates.

## Journal -> Clip distribution

1. Publish a completed story through the **owned Journal** with an actual publisher receipt.
2. Verify that the canonical HTTPS URL resolves with the correct headline, story ID, OpenGraph metadata and public article content.
3. Build distinct pieces for Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok/Shorts, X and other authorized company pages. Different readers and formats deserve different treatments, not one identical copied block.
4. Publish through **Evercraft Clip** or another explicitly authorized owned distribution surface. A queued post, prepared caption, handoff or API acceptance is not proof of publication.
5. Record actual provider receipt or live permalink per destination. Mark each destination **published**, **held**, **failed** or **unverified** independently.
6. Do not require a new manual approval for every low-risk, pre-authorized post when the existing governed release is already valid. Do not bypass required high-risk/safety/rights reviews.
7. If the Journal is unavailable, use only the existing authorized independent-source fallback with verified sources and a genuine signed release. Never insert a nonexistent Journal URL.

## Editorial distribution patterns

- **Journal:** complete story and citations.
- **Facebook:** four developed paragraphs, conversational but substantial; Journal link in the appropriate placement for the account's distribution convention.
- **Instagram:** original image or short factual carousel; useful caption that stands on its own and invites curiosity.
- **LinkedIn:** observation, implication, limit, discussion question; credibility above novelty.
- **TikTok/Reels/Shorts:** original motion, clear narration, measured claims, source and distinction between illustration and observation.
- **X/Bluesky:** one self-contained insight within account limits, link when the release exists.
- **Comments:** engage intelligently with questions; do not bait disputes or mock misunderstandings.

## Healthy gravity signals

Track not only impressions, but:
- verified Journal article publications per week;
- verified provider publications per channel;
- article views and returning readers, where measurable;
- non-follower reach, saves, thoughtful comments and completed video views;
- readers who read a second story or subscribe for future stories;
- inbound curiosity, collaboration and eventual genuinely attributable commercial leads.

Do not manufacture traffic or call drafts, schedules, impressions or engagement actual sales. Low engagement is feedback, not justification for sensational claims.
