## What the research changed

Conversational naturalness requires a contingent relationship between the speakers, not simply two timbres reading alternating cards. See:
- Sacks, Schegloff, Jefferson 1974 (turn-taking, https://doi.org/10.2307/412243)
- Stivers et al. 2009 (turn transition distributions, https://cognitionandculture.net/wp-content/uploads/Stivers_2009_universals.pdf)
- Schegloff, Jefferson, Sacks 1977 (repair/self-correction, https://www.jstor.org/stable/413107)
- Turn-taking and repair timing study (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4357221/)
- Paierl et al. 2025 (backchannel timing, https://www.mdpi.com/2226-471X/10/8/194)
- CANDOR naturalistic corpus (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10065445/)

## Structured interdisciplinary council findings

Conversation analyst: an action must update both speakers' common understanding.
Script editor: organize into initiation -> instruction -> mistaken action -> "Wait?" repair -> explanation -> owner's self-attribution -> acknowledgment.
Acting coach: human moves calm -> disbelief -> recognition -> new boundary. Agent stays concise and responsive, not cheery.
Dialogue designer: write sentences a human would really say, including contractions and direct reference to a prior statement.
Vocal engineer: audition full utterances with unchanged words, never patch phonemes or digitally speed through an uncomfortable take.
Sound editor: measured dialogue gaps, almost no music under surprise and correction; agent may have a plausible laptop acoustic perspective.
Cinematic editor: show the phone/client message while the owner processes it, then place their self-attribution on the permission record as the next action begins.
Truth/legal: no live-product or automatic legal liability claims, do not treat pretrained Kokoro as owned model weights or approved commercial vocal identity.
QA: blind audio-only A/B, check active listening, response timing, lexical stress, natural human reaction; inspect actual media bytes and human listen before release.
