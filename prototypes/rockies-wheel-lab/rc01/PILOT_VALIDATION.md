## Market reality (competitive gate)

- Discount Tire already offers a vehicle-specific wheel visualizer and compatibility shopping. https://www.discounttire.com/wheels
- WheelPeek explicitly markets a browser-based white-label/iPad/QR wheel try-on and retailer catalog/feed integrations to wheel shops. https://wheelpeek.com/business/
- WheelPeek's own consumer beta also advertises photoreal rendering, an aftermarket catalog and manufacturer-data-based preliminary fitment; its site says actual fitment should still be seller verified. https://wheelpeek.com/

**Therefore:** “a wheel visualizer” is **not a unique product**. Evaluate whether a lightweight, privacy-conscious, shop-owned quoting/lead handoff (or licensing an existing visualization provider rather than duplicating its technology) would solve a *verified* merchant problem. Make no new AI-fitment, exact-photo, or inventory claims for current RC02.

## Written-first discovery protocol (owner approval before sends)

Suggested opt-in message, **unsent**:

> Hey, I'm building a simple interactive wheel/customization concept for local auto shops and wanted some real-world feedback before taking it further. We have a working appearance-only demo, not a fitment or inventory integration. Do your customers ever struggle to visualize wheel upgrades, and would a customer-generated visual spec help your quote process? Happy to send a link or screenshots if you'd like to take a look. No obligation.

Only after a willing shop responds, ask:

1. What part of choosing wheels currently causes the most hesitation or repeated work?
2. How do you show wheel options today, including distributors' visualizers? Which existing tools already solve this?
3. How is a wheel's actual vehicle fitment checked, and who has authority over inventory/price?
4. Would a customer-shared comparison card or a shop-branded QR preview materially reduce quote time? What would you *stop doing* if it worked?
5. Would you test a private, clearly labeled concept proof? What measurable outcome, consent, and monthly budget would make a paid version worthwhile?

## Go/no-go evidence gate

- **Discovery:** at least three *independent* willingly obtained merchant responses, with dates and consent path, not social likes.
- **Differentiation:** a documented pain that Discount Tire/WheelPeek/current distributor tools fail to solve for that exact buyer.
- **Pilot:** at least one explicit shop opt-in identifying point of contact, scope, data rights and their chosen success metric.
- **Product truth:** no visual appearance treated as engineering fitment; no stock/price without merchant-supplied authoritative evidence.
- **Commercial:** payment terms only after measurable buyer value; route via Systemia and Evercraft Payments with independent verification.
- **Release:** mission admission, permitted data contracts, security/privacy/accessibility QA, licensed visual assets, founder publication gate and final receipt.

**Stop or redirect:** if shops already have sufficient visualization, consider services that implement or improve existing tools instead of competing in image-rendering. An integration or marketing-service pilot may produce revenue with much less custom engineering.
