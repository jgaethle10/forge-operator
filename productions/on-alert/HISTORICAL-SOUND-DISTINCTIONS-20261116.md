# ON ALERT | Sound and historical specificity | November 16, 2026

**Founding creative direction:** life inside the Cold War missile system, especially the ordinary people who worked there and the tension between everyday routines and catastrophic responsibility.

## Correction to visual/audio assumptions
Do NOT portray a Minuteman launch-control capsule as a loud, fuel-saturated Titan/Atlas industrial space. Minuteman used solid-fuel missiles, with weapons in separate silos and two-person crews operating a hardened control capsule. A contemporary **1974 Air Force Magazine** account explicitly describes the launch control capsule as surprisingly quiet: low equipment hum and occasional radio voices rather than constant industrial roar. The same account documents crew arrival, elevator, blast door, study, and waiting.

**Primary source:** Lt. Ralph H. Hallquist, USAF, “Behind the Blast Doors,” *Air Force Magazine*, October 1974, pp. 58–59. https://www.airandspaceforces.com/app/uploads/2024/09/AFmag_1974_10.pdf

### Separate environments
- Minuteman control capsule: subdued electronics/communication and alert routines.
- Field maintenance and generator equipment: variable industrial noise; verify exact facility.
- Atlas and Titan launch sites: liquid-fueled missile hazards; do not generalize to solid-fueled Minuteman.

### Narration concept (original, not quoted testimony)
“Above ground, the landscape was ordinary. Beyond the blast door, even the silence had a job.”
