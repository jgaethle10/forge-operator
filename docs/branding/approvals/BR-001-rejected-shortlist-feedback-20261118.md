## Critique, distinguished from founder quote
The founder's comment is a direct verdict, but offers no granular explanation. These are *working hypotheses*, not claims about why the founder rejected the concepts:
- Invented names do not independently communicate professional commercial value or an ownable, memorable business story.
- Small SVG pictograms in rounded cards are not finished brand symbols.
- The creative ideas need to be judged at realistic customer touchpoints (hero, commercial, social collateral, invoice context), not only by logo/name samples.
- Brand design should take its personality from the actual buyer, market and service promise. We should not keep adding random compound names just to fill a shortlist.

## Next iteration design brief
1. Begin with customer reality: this is a scoped, $100 week of help for a busy small-business owner. Discover why the service is worth purchasing and how a buyer should describe it to someone else.
2. Establish the product's **point of view**, emotional character, competitive context and trust signals before naming.
3. Produce distinct **full brand systems**, not a collection of names with generic icons: product story, art direction, typography system, wordmark, original distinctive mark, landing-page hero and example customer-facing collateral.
4. Test spoken name, immediate service clarity, readability at mobile sizes, legibility, confusion risk, and basic public-use signals. Do not imply official trademark clearance.
5. Show one thoughtfully argued creative direction at a time, with a meaningful alternative only if it solves a different strategic positioning question. Do not flood the founder with novelty words.
6. Require explicit founder acceptance of particular elements; record exactly which parts were liked or rejected in an append-only review result. No automatic approvals from "push".
