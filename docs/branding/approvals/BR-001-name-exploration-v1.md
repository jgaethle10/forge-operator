## Why the naming round was opened
The first reviewed name, The Weekly Signal, overlaps with already-published newsletter identities, including a marketing editorial series. That creates a practical confusion risk. This is a source-backed naming concern, not a determination of trademark infringement. The original BR-001.v1 packet remains intact; this file supplements it.

## Preliminary search and exclusions
A limited web search for exact candidate names surfaced existing uses for some of the more obvious options. Those were removed **before** showing a shortlist:

- **The Weekly Signal**: Multiple newsletter/editorial uses including marketing. Source: https://mktrintheloop.com/p/introducing-the-weekly-signal
- **Weekloom**: Existing commercial weekly planner product. Source: https://weekloom.com/
- **Poststead**: Existing subscription digest publishing product. Source: https://poststead.com/
- **Weekward**: Existing iPhone lifestyle app. Source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/weekward-life-in-weeks/id6792265603
- **Weekmark**: Established brand with foreign trademark registration in multiple service classes. Source: https://www.portalchile.org/detalle-marca/weekmark-1054886
- **Weeklift**: Existing SEO reporting product. Source: https://weeklift.com/

The final three phrases below did not reveal obvious exact-name companies in the limited queries I ran. That does not mean they are available, trademark-safe, registrable, domain-free or unique across social networks. A real clearance step remains required.

## Three independently authored working directions

### 1. Weekkin: Warm and human
**Line:** “Present, even when you're busy.”  
**Promise:** Your business deserves to be heard, even on the busiest week.  
**Purpose:** Give busy neighborhood-scale businesses a reliable, manageable week of social media support.  
**Voice:** Warm, neighborly, observant, complete sentences; no cute baby talk, no fake promises

**Narrative:** Running a small business already asks for a full day. Then there's the public side of it: the update that never gets written, the project people never hear about, and the post sitting in drafts. Weekkin is the idea of a capable extra hand for one agreed week, without turning a small problem into a long retainer.

**Art direction:** Editorial photography of real small-business work, soft cream copy panels, two gently overlapping rectangular speech tiles. Colors: foundation `#17252a`, panel `#243c3d`, text `#f9f2e6`, primary `#f1bb8c` and supporting `#a9dbc9`.

**Proposed symbol:** paired offset speech tiles with a shared baseline

**Best argument:** Friendly and independently ownable-sounding word; centers companionship rather than algorithm hacks

**Important weakness:** Invented name does not communicate social marketing on its own; needs service descriptor. Search coverage is not legal clearance.

**Working heuristic ratings (NOT customer research or legal scoring):** Distinctiveness 4/5; clarity 2/5; human fit 5/5; ability to expand beyond one package 4/5.


### 2. Cadencekeep: Modern and operational
**Line:** “Consistency without the extra overhead.”  
**Promise:** One good week of steady, approved communication.  
**Purpose:** Help small organizations sustain a credible publishing rhythm within a finite, agreed weekly scope.  
**Voice:** Composed, concise, reliable, businesslike, not agency-jargon heavy

**Narrative:** Every business has something worth saying, but a scattered posting schedule makes it hard for people to notice. Cadencekeep is built around an attainable rhythm: agree what matters, create or manage the approved work, hand back a clearer next week. The goal is consistency, not a promise of viral growth.

**Art direction:** Structured cobalt grid, chartlike rhythm motif, acid-lime signal markers, strong sans typography. Colors: foundation `#111f31`, panel `#1b3245`, text `#f4f6ed`, primary `#c6d55e` and supporting `#afc7d5`.

**Proposed symbol:** four offset vertical beats plus a closed continuation line

**Best argument:** Communicates rhythm and long-term consistency; more professional than the first direction

**Important weakness:** Slightly long to say and type; compound may not feel elegant to a buyer.

**Working heuristic ratings (NOT customer research or legal scoring):** Distinctiveness 4/5; clarity 3/5; human fit 3/5; ability to expand beyond one package 4/5.


### 3. Weekmorrow: Optimistic and story-led
**Line:** “The week ahead starts here.”  
**Promise:** A useful week of content. A better starting point tomorrow.  
**Purpose:** Help an owner regain momentum through one scoped week of thoughtful social support and a usable handoff.  
**Voice:** Optimistic, thoughtful, unhurried and plainspoken; no miracle language

**Narrative:** The days get busy, and communication slides to tomorrow. Weekmorrow turns that vague tomorrow into one practical week: choose the message, agree on the work and approvals, and hand off something useful. It is about forward progress, not pretending a week can transform a whole company.

**Art direction:** Warm dawn horizon, paper textures made with CSS, calm editorial serif and honest work photography. Colors: foundation `#1e2930`, panel `#293f43`, text `#fff2df`, primary `#f5ad6e` and supporting `#b4d4b4`.

**Proposed symbol:** sun crossing a seven-day horizon, two rising lines

**Best argument:** Strong narrative arc and possible visual storytelling; future-forward without sounding enterprise-only

**Important weakness:** Can sound like a planner or lifestyle brand; may need obvious service descriptor on every page.

**Working heuristic ratings (NOT customer research or legal scoring):** Distinctiveness 4/5; clarity 2/5; human fit 4/5; ability to expand beyond one package 3/5.



## Creative working recommendation, not approval
**Weekkin** has the strongest human small-business story of these three. It positions the service as tangible help rather than marketing wizardry or newsletter content. However, the trade-off is explicit: *Weekkin* does not tell a first-time viewer what is sold. Every market-facing rendering needs a nearby descriptor such as “one-week social media support for small businesses.”

**Do not treat this recommendation as a founder vote.** The name also has not passed a US trademark, domain or social-handle clearance process.
