## WRSA-I inventory and transfer reconciliation | additional public-record review, 2026-11-21

## Transfer authority: enacted 2024 law versus proposed 2026 changes

**April 24, 2024 was a genuine legal pivot.** Division A of **Public Law 118-50**, §305, amended the preexisting Israel-specific WRSA transfer authority in §12001 of the FY2005 DoD Appropriations Act [36]. The changes were:

- **Article eligibility:** substitutes broadly described DoD-inventory articles intended for and located in the Israel stockpile at transfer for the former item list beginning “armor” that previously focused on obsolete/surplus categories. This is a change to eligible articles; it is not itself a license for unauthorized battlefield transfer.
- **Consideration:** substituted compensation “in an amount to be determined by the Secretary of Defense” for the previous requirement that consideration be at least fair-market value. This is statutory pricing authority, **not proof any particular transfer was given free or at a below-market price**.
- **Notice timing:** permits the President, case-by-case during extraordinary circumstances impacting U.S. national security, to notify Congress **as far in advance of transfer as practicable**, instead of only the preexisting advance-period approach.
- **FY2024 stockpile additions:** §306 expressly turned off §514(b)'s addition cap **for fiscal year 2024**; it did not create an unlimited permanent addition authority. Also, **stockpile addition is distinct from transferring stockpile title to Israel**.

**Critical timeline and counterarguments:** These enacted April 2024 changes should not be projected backward onto emergency case **24-15 (December 2023)**. The DoD proposal dated **May 29, 2026** asking for later 2029/2032 expiration dates is a separate **proposed** change [33], not evidence Congress accepted those requested expiration dates. Any claim that emergency notification means no legal process at all would be wrong. A transfer may still require lawful authorization, title accounting and applicable safeguards. A completed transfer is not established by a public notification alone.

**Contract-side corroboration:** SOC's Jan. 26, 2022 contemporaneous press announcement described a ten-year **$220M award** for protecting U.S. personnel in Jerusalem, West Bank and Gaza [37]. The State Department OIG independently confirms SOC as Jerusalem **TO-3** vendor ($202.48M initial award) [1]; USAspending and award metadata identify **19AQMM22F0090** as SOC's Jerusalem contract [2,4]. The contract identifier crosswalk remains formally unresolved. These numbers represent public announcements/award authorizations/obligations/outlays, not interchangeable expenditure figures.
