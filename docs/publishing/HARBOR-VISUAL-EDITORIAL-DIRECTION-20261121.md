# Harbor Visual Direction | The story before the template
**Founder direction:** November 21, 2026 (Pacific)  
**Applies to:** New Evercraft Journal, ESAN, Spider Society, Learning Network and other approved Page editorial packages.  
**Status:** Editorial direction to be acknowledged by Harbor; not evidence that Harbor's live publishing runtime adopted it. Existing rights, editorial, destination and provider gates remain in force.

## The creative north star
Make the reader curious about **the actual subject**. The image should give a useful first impression before the caption is read. We want understated, sophisticated, credible editorial design, **not a futuristic AI spectacle**. Think a well-produced science magazine, documentary still, sports desk or investigative newspaper depending on the subject.

**One image, one clear idea.** Show the story rather than announcing the brand. Keep Evercraft recognizable in a small, consistent signature, without forcing every brand and topic into the same navy-and-yellow headline rectangle.

## How Harbor chooses the visual (in this order)
1. **Best:** Specific, authentic, rights-cleared event/person/place/object photograph, field footage, official source image or original media. Verify origin, date, permissions, context and credit; never repurpose a real photo to imply an event it does not depict.
2. **When the story is evidence or data:** A clean *sourced* chart, original map, comparison, timeline, diagram, or carefully cropped authentic source document. Use real numbers and labels. Draw only relationships the evidence supports; distinguish observation from interpretation.
3. **When reality cannot be pictured responsibly:** A simple, elegant original *labeled illustration*, typography-led scientific diagram, or conceptual artwork. Avoid fabricated documentary photos, fake people, fake real-world incidents, invented devices, generic hooded hackers, mystical glowing brains and random data strands.
4. **Last resort:** A handsome, spare headline card with meaningful hierarchy when media cannot be sourced or an urgent public-interest item needs immediate publication. This is a valid fallback, not the default look of the whole network. If images cannot be cleared, source-linked text is better than an invented photograph.

## The look and the restraint
- **Focal point first:** One compelling subject dominates. A genuine photo or useful illustration occupies most of the canvas; typography supports it, never suffocates it.
- **Less copy:** Prefer a short, specific headline. Small subtitles and dates must survive phone viewing; if they cannot be read, simplify rather than shrinking them further. Keep the whole caption in the post, not on the image.
- **Brand is a signature:** Small consistent logo/label, tasteful typography and spacing. Retain each Page's identity without repeating identical rectangles across unrelated beats. Brand color should accent the story, not overpower it.
- **No default AI decoration:** No glows, circuitry, particle trails, sci-fi HUDs, artificial lens flares, needless hex grids, or busy composites unless such graphics genuinely explain the subject and are clearly identified.
- **No artificial urgency:** Avoid danger colors, alarm labels and 'breaking' styling for routine or historical items. Respect source confidence, chronology and the gravity of the event.
- **Phone-first crop:** Confirm the full subject and essential text within a 4:5 feed creative and a 9:16 adaptation where actually needed. Check at common phone size before release. No unlicensed images, mislabeling or unverifiable source credits.

## The lightweight decision before a post
Harbor's visual choice should answer **four questions** in the existing story package, not create a new bureaucracy:

1. **What is the one thing the viewer should see or understand?** Name the subject, not the brand.
2. **What is this image really?** Authentic photograph, original evidence chart/map, source-document excerpt, conceptual illustration, or fallback type card. Record source, date, rights/credit and truthful label.
3. **Does it pass the phone glance test?** Strong focal point, legible text, no crop loss, no unsupported impression. If not, simplify or pick a better medium.
4. **Is this distinct from the last several images on this Page and the wider network?** If it's another identical navy headline block, reconsider. No forced rotation or quotas; choose quality first.

**Release:** Existing Harbor/Systemia editorial, visual-rights and Meta authorization gates still decide whether a finished package can post. Do not edit, delete, re-share or replace already-live posts without their own approval. Maintain the current high-value publishing cadence; improve new posts progressively instead of pausing the newsroom to redesign everything.

**Learning:** For the next **five new editorial posts across distinct beats**, record selected medium, specific story fit, source/rights state, phone preview, and actual provider receipt. Compare real engagement only when available; do not invent performance, require a new live system, or call an offline standards document a runtime rollout.

**Connections, not new infrastructure:** Use existing Visual Desk for meaningful maps/charts, existing Fallen production for larger creative work, and Harbor's current approved social workflow for post and provider receipts.
