# Evercraft Client-Facing Simplicity Standard
Founder direction: October 28, 2026.

**Rule:** Evercraft may operate an extremely sophisticated Systemia, Saban and agent ecosystem internally. A customer should see only the shortest understandable path from their problem to a useful result. This is a permanent release principle for websites, onboarding, product UIs, customer emails, checkout and deliverables.

## The customer-facing contract
1. **One sentence says what the product does**, using the customer's vocabulary.
2. **One concrete example** shows the expected outcome. It must be labeled if illustrative or synthetic.
3. **One clear next action** in the main viewport. Avoid a menu of equally weighted CTAs.
4. **Three simple steps at most** to explain the customer journey before deeper optional information.
5. **Use progressive disclosure.** Trust/legal details, specifications, audit receipts, mission traces and advanced controls are accessible on demand, not in the hero. Never remove required consent or material pricing/terms.
6. **Be honest about readiness.** Only deployed capabilities are described as ready. Do not use animated pretend execution or claim sales from checkout redirects.
7. **Respect attention.** Mobile first, high contrast, readable typography, responsive design, minimal dependencies, no auto-playing media, no forced calls, no excessive signup questions.
8. **One real task-to-result demonstration** is worth more than ten feature lists. Only real permissioned customer data may enter production workloads.
9. **Measure usability and conversion.** Test that a new visitor can identify the outcome and next action in seconds. Track qualified intent, paid checkout, verified delivery, and repeat customer separately.
10. **Respect brand differentiation.** Each product may have a distinct palette. Work Saban's selected direction: mostly white (#FFFFFF), crimson (#A4142F), black (#171717), and soft white (#F8F8F7). This palette is not mandatory across every Evercraft product.

## Work Saban implementation
- Public homepage: “Give your work a team.” 
- One example: monthly sales report.
- One primary CTA: join private pilot by written inquiry, until a production-approved paid offer exists.
- The public preview is deliberately minimal and illustrative. The local engineering Work Floor, worker traces and proof panels live behind the product, not in the customer pitch.
- When Work Saban becomes production-ready, its customer screen should begin with a single task entry, a clear result, and optional “View team” or “Inspect evidence” panels. Never make people navigate the internal Systemia operating model to use the product.
- Keep separate engineering issue #1630 and PR #1629 readiness gates. Simplifying customer-facing copy must not weaken consent, authorization, auditing, security or financial proof.

## Review question
Before a customer-facing release, ask: **Could a tired person understand what to do next without reading how our systems work?**

If not, simplify it.
