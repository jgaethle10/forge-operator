What the model sees right now

The first five positions in this edition are #1 Alabama, #2 Notre Dame, #3 Texas, #4 Texas Tech, #5 Indiana. This is not a ballot and it is not designed to copy one. The ordering comes from the season ledger attached to ranking run cfbpi:c0a0e97d3356b3d52d57c04f. Completed results are adjusted for opponent strength and designated venue, scoring margins are capped so one demolition cannot own an entire résumé, and the most recent games matter without erasing the rest of the season. The top line is therefore a snapshot of demonstrated team strength through the evidence cutoff, not a prediction that the current order will survive the next Saturday.

Why Alabama is No. 1

Alabama is 5-0 and owns a Power Score of 81.40 with 95 percent model confidence. Its current component profile combines a result-quality score of 80.8, opponent-adjusted efficiency of 91.5, schedule strength of 62.1 and recent-form score of 81.0. The important part is not any single number. The lead survives several independent pieces of the current results ledger rather than depending on poll reputation or one headline win.

Where uncertainty still lives

This edition deliberately leaves availability and turnover-sustainability inputs unfilled when they are not backed by a governed evidence feed. Missing information is not converted into zero and modeled values are not labeled as observations. Every team therefore carries an explicit confidence value and a missing-component list in the machine-readable ranking receipt. That makes this edition more conservative than a ranking that quietly guesses at injuries, quarterback availability or turnover luck, and it gives future versions a clean place to improve as those evidence lanes become reliable.

What happens next

The ranking is designed to move when the evidence moves. Each new completed-game ledger can be replayed against the same versioned model, compared with the prior published edition and scored later against what happened on the field. Future editions can add verified player availability, deeper efficiency measures and consensus-poll comparison without rewriting history. The goal is not to win an argument on publication day. It is to build a public record showing where Evercraft was early, where it was wrong and whether the model becomes more predictive as the season develops.

What remains uncertain

  • Player availability is not yet included unless a governed evidence feed is added.
  • Turnover sustainability is intentionally missing in this edition rather than inferred.
  • NCAA scoreboard data may designate home and away without exposing neutral-site state for every game.
  • This is a power ranking derived from completed results, not a playoff selection projection.
