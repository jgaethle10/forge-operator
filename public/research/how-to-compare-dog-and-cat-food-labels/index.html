How to compare dog and cat food labels without relying on marketing claims

A practical evidence-first checklist for comparing pet-food labels, nutritional adequacy and life-stage claims.

Not yet search-indexed by design. This evidence-linked overview is an editorial preview awaiting Systemia review, source/provenance checks and public-edge verification. It is not a claim of an earlier completed Evercraft research report.

Begin with nutritional adequacy, not a fashionable ingredient

A label's nutritional adequacy statement is more informative than a catchy ingredient claim. Look for language indicating that the food is complete and balanced for the intended species and life stage. AAFCO explains that complete means required nutrients are present, while balanced refers to their ratios.

Match the food to the animal

A puppy, kitten, pregnant animal or working dog may have different nutrition needs from a less-active adult. A dog-food formula should not be treated as nutritionally interchangeable with cat food. Compare the stated species and life stage before comparing individual ingredients or package claims.

Check how and how much to feed

Read the calorie-content and feeding-direction statements. Feeding amounts are starting points, not personalized prescriptions. Track body condition and discuss meaningful changes, chronic conditions or therapeutic diets with a veterinarian. Treats and supplements generally are not substitutes for a complete daily diet.

What this research does not establish

These label-reading rules do not rank brands or prove that one ingredient list is healthiest for a particular animal. No product-specific nutritional analysis, testing, recall check or individualized veterinary assessment was performed for this overview.
