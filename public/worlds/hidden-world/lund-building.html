ARCHITECTURE FILE 001 · YAKIMA, WASHINGTON

A building with
more than one life.

The Lund Building stood at North Front Street and East Yakima Avenue in 1899. Its original entrance, storefront renovations, stonework and neighboring doorways survive in a remarkable documentary record. Walk through the evidence, not an invented tunnel.

1898–1899 · Saloon period

Alfalfa Saloon

Thomas Lund commissioned Charles Bruenn to design the building; A. F. Switzer built it for approximately $4,000. The historic nomination describes an original diagonal corner entrance and retractable awnings. The cut-corner geometry shown here is symbolic, not a measured floor plan.

Around 1910 · Retail conversion

Chicago Clothing Company

After the saloon, the first floor was converted to retail use with large plate-glass storefront windows. The upper story retained the cut-corner form, while the lower storefront changed. The illustrated glass layout is not a measured copy.

1920s · Exterior change

A new marquee

The National Register description notes that a metal marquee was added to the Yakima Avenue facade during the 1920s. This diagram shows a generic band to indicate that documented change. Its original precise shape and year remain unverified here.

1983 · Historical documentation

Preserved, but altered

The nomination describes two stories, load-bearing local stone walls, segmentally arched upper windows and a first-floor ceiling about 14 feet high. It also states that interior doorways connected the building to two neighbors, without establishing a date for those connections or an underground route.

DOCUMENTED · STRUCTURE

What we can say

  • Two-story structure with load-bearing local stone walls.
  • Original 1899 saloon entrance at a truncated corner.
  • Large retail glazing introduced around 1910.
  • Segmentally arched second-story windows.
  • Approximately 14-foot first-floor ceiling.
  • Metal marquee added during the 1920s.
UNKNOWN · RECONSTRUCTION

What we cannot say

  • Exact footprint, elevations or door positions for a chosen year.
  • Which neighboring buildings were connected at a given date.
  • Any mapped basement-to-basement connection.
  • Whether a surviving tunnel network exists beneath this property.

These need original plans, Sanborn sheets, property records and authorized building research.
