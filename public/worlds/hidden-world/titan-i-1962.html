COLD WAR FILE 001 · LARSON AIR FORCE BASE REGION

The world beneath
the launch field.

Between 1959 and 1965, three vast underground Titan I complexes near Moses Lake brought the Cold War beneath Washington's surface. Explore the history, structural ideas and human work behind them, with the original evidence always one tap away.

December 1, 1959 · ground broken

The land opened

Construction of the three Larson AFB-area Titan I complexes began in late 1959. Contemporary descriptions emphasize the scale of the hardened underground works. The cranes and open excavation depicted here are symbolic, not a reconstruction of a particular Washington worksite.

1962 · operational

Three missiles, one buried complex

Each of Washington's Titan I complexes housed three missiles in deep reinforced silos, together with an underground control room and associated facilities. This abstract section depicts their relationship but does not reproduce the location, dimensions or exact plan of a real site.

January 1, 1965 · stood down

An underground era ends

The Titan I installations stood down at the start of 1965 and were deactivated in March. Salvage operations removed valuable equipment. The retired complexes entered private ownership; none is presented here as a public place to explore.

Present · closed and hazardous

The structures outlasted their mission

These historical facilities survive in altered and potentially hazardous conditions. The Quincy/Royal City complex is reported to be flooded. All site markers in the regional atlas identify nearby towns only, not entrances or coordinates of these properties.

INTERACTIVE ENGINEERING NOTES

What made it a buried world?

Hardened chambers. Titan I missiles sat in substantial below-ground silos. At a high level, the historic engineering challenge was sheltering a large launch system and then bringing it to the surface. The diagram is not designed to recreate mechanical operation.

Human presence underground. A buried launch-control room made this more than storage space. Crews worked beneath the ground in a high-pressure Cold War environment. We have not modeled the actual crew quarters, personnel passages or control equipment.

One interdependent facility. Official historical accounts describe multiple underground spaces linked by tunnels and utility infrastructure. Their existence is documented, but the connections in our graphic are not measured passageways or directions through any complex.

ACCESS / SAFETY / PROVENANCE

Explore the history, not the hazard.

All three former Washington Titan I complexes are closed to visitors. One has been reported flooded. No entrance directions, current owner identification, site coordinates, or real passage layout are supplied here. This exhibit does not grant access to any structure.
