Save the software before you rebuild it

A broken system can be loud enough to make replacement feel obvious. Start smaller: reproduce the failure, map what still works, identify the expensive break, establish authorization and rollback boundaries, then test whether a bounded repair can restore reliability.

Evidence before architecture opinions

A broken checkout does not prove an entire application is unsalvageable. An abandoned codebase does not prove it has no useful business logic. A fragile AI-built app does not automatically require a rewrite. Separate observed symptoms from root-cause hypotheses.

What to preserve

Inventory customer flows, data, integrations, business rules, search visibility, reporting, and other working behavior before touching the system.

When rebuilding earns its case

A rebuild becomes easier to justify when evidence shows systemic unsupported dependencies, unsafe assumptions, repeated unrelated regressions, or repair economics that no longer make sense.
