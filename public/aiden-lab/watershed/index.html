LOOK UNDER THE LANDSCAPE

Where does it go?

Snow: At below-freezing zone temperatures, precipitation is stored as snow-water equivalent. Warmer temperatures melt existing snow.

Soil: Liquid water infiltrates until the simplified infiltration rate or soil capacity is reached. Vegetation raises both in this model.

River: Excess surface water reaches the river quickly. Groundwater releases more slowly. Plants and warm air remove some soil water as evaporation and transpiration.

Altitude experiment: Drag a ridge while sculpt mode is on, or use the elevation buttons. This model lowers zone air temperature by a fixed 6.5°C per kilometer above the valley; that changes simulated rain, snowfall and thaw. The lapse rate is an illustrative assumption, not a real local measurement.

Reality check: Five equal-area zones, constant daily weather, fixed soil properties and no routing delays. This is a transparent learning model, not a calibrated watershed, flood prediction or official hydrology dataset.

FOLLOW A MYSTERY

Three investigations. Infinite variations.

The storm that became a river

Make a mountain storm. Does the river rise as the soil fills? Compare the same storm on gentle and steep terrain.

The mountain remembers winter

Start with stored snow. Warm the valley, then watch meltwater travel through the basin.

Bring the forest back

Compare bare soil with a forest under identical rainfall. Does more infiltration change the river output?
