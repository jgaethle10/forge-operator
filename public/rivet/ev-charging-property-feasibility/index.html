Is This Property a Good Fit for EV Charging?

EV charging feasibility is not a single score. It is a stack of questions about who would charge, when they would stay, what charging alternatives already exist, how power would be served, what the property can physically support, and whether the modeled economics justify deeper diligence.

Property owners often encounter broad EV adoption statistics that say little about a specific site. A useful feasibility screen narrows the question to the actual location and labels each input by evidence state so public facts, inferred signals, modeled assumptions, and unknowns do not blur together.

What a site-specific screen should examine

  • user and vehicle demand signals tied to the location
  • existing charger supply and competitive gaps
  • likely dwell-time pattern for the property type
  • utility, tariff, and demand-charge context
  • parking, access, visibility, and preliminary site-fit considerations
  • incentive programs that may warrant eligibility review
  • economics scenarios rather than a guaranteed return

What should feasibility mean?

It should mean enough site-specific evidence to decide whether the property deserves deeper engineering, utility coordination, incentive verification, and commercial planning. It should not masquerade as final design approval.

Evidence boundaries

Missing data is not zero. Mapped charger inventory is not observed utilization. Program presence is not proof of site eligibility. Modeled economics are not guaranteed realized economics. Preliminary screening and planning are not final engineering, survey, permitting, utility interconnection, financing, or construction approval.
