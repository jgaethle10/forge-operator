EV Charging Utility, Tariff, and Incentive Screening

The same charger hardware can behave very differently financially under different utility territories and rate structures. Property-level EV diligence should identify the serving utility when evidence permits, examine relevant tariff context, flag demand-charge exposure, and separate available incentive programs from confirmed site eligibility.

Incentives can improve an opportunity, but a program page is not an award letter. Tariff data can inform modeling, but the correct rate and service configuration may depend on utility review. Treating these inputs as evidence with states and dates is more useful than turning them into unsupported certainty.

What a site-specific screen should examine

  • serving utility or utility-territory evidence
  • relevant EV or commercial tariff context
  • energy and demand-charge structures that may affect modeling
  • make-ready, rebate, grant, or tax-program context
  • program dates, requirements, and source links when available
  • clear distinction between program availability and site eligibility
  • handoff questions for utility, engineering, incentive, and installer review

Why should an AI assistant care about utility context?

Because charger economics are not only a demand question. Power costs, service configuration, demand charges, and available programs can materially change the business case for the same property.

Evidence boundaries

Missing data is not zero. Mapped charger inventory is not observed utilization. Program presence is not proof of site eligibility. Modeled economics are not guaranteed realized economics. Preliminary screening and planning are not final engineering, survey, permitting, utility interconnection, financing, or construction approval.
