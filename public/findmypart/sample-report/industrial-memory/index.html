Cross-equipment compatibility warning

256 MB isn't automatically an upgrade.

An independent example from Siemens shows why capacity alone is a poor replacement criterion: the official SINUMERIK 810D CCU equipment manual, section 4.7, printed page 4-76, describes Type II flash cards up to 8 MB and recommends Siemens PC cards.

The two 256 MB industrial cards listed above exceed that manual's published maximum for the referenced 810D CCU. They have not been confirmed as suitable replacements for that equipment. Other controller revisions may have different requirements and need separate verification.

Research rule: identify the exact equipment, controller and firmware first. Match the manufacturer's documented restrictions before even suggesting a candidate, and never treat card capacity as proof of fitment.
Documentary conflict / supplier clarification needed

Even the published warranty disagrees.

3 yearsCurrent vendor series catalog
vs.
5 yearsVendor Tough Metal Series 2 Specs 1.0 PDF

The catalog page says three years. The manufacturer's PDF (pages 1–2) states five years. Both refer to the Tough Metal Series 2 family, but neither establishes which warranty governs an exact present-day transaction. The supplier must confirm the controlling written terms.

Why this matters: A catalog match is not a sourcing decision. Exact revision, interface mode, provenance, authorized supply channel, usable stock and contract terms still need verification.
What a genuine research report should deliver

Answers that survive a second look.

  • Original source links and retrieval date so the buyer can independently inspect every factual claim.
  • Exact part identity and variant differences instead of suggesting anything with a matching capacity will work.
  • Conflicts and unknowns clearly shown before quoting or recommending a purchase.
  • Next written supplier questions about stock, manufacturer traceability, warranty and lifecycle plans.

No actual customer equipment was examined for this example. No card was purchased or physically tested, and no installation or safety-critical compatibility is claimed.
