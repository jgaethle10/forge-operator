# Evercraft Aqua | WaterFarm-001
## Research partnership concept — public, non-confidential, v0.1 (24 October 2026)

**Who:** Evercraft LLC, a Yakima-based technology and engineering company. This document is an invitation to discuss research, not evidence of a completed partnership.

**Problem.** Agriculture needs water-security solutions that are economical, measurable, safe and adapted to the site. General claims about “creating water from air” are inadequate. An effective pilot must count *net freshwater withdrawn*, *energy*, *water-quality constraints*, and *total cost*, without treating water internally recycled within a greenhouse as newly created regional supply.

**Hypothesis.** A modular water-accounting platform can help compare existing and novel water supply/recovery approaches at a specific site:
1. **Permitted rooftop precipitation collection** into isolated nonpotable storage, where legally appropriate.
2. **Enclosed crop-environment condensate recovery** with calibrated measurements. The experiment must measure whether incremental electricity and maintenance costs are justified and whether crop conditions are affected.
3. **Future, separately approved** treatment/reuse interfaces, subject to licensed design, water quality, permits and independently validated feasibility.

**What already exists:** a versioned, advisory-only Python mass-balance simulation with unit tests and explicit default-deny flags for unapproved collection, condensate reuse and nonpotable delivery. It does not control physical equipment, validate water chemistry or demonstrate field water savings. Code lives in a draft review branch of Evercraft's GitHub repository.

**Proposed low-risk study:** Select one willing, authorized host with existing water-use data. Collect baseline demand and site conditions. Design a 14-day matched experiment or longer where researchers advise necessary sampling. Independently meter inputs, captured water, storage, returned flows, delivered water, incremental energy and disposal. Compare with the simplest compliant and lower-cost option. No system will route experimental condensate to crops or drinking supplies without appropriate treatment, quality verification and authorization.

**Partner requests:** feedback on scientific design and suitable crop environments; a prospective site host; equipment/measurement expertise; treatment-quality and water-right guidance. A short email exchange with an appropriate technical lead is enough for a first step. We are not requesting unconditional funding, immediate construction, facility access without permission, or a technology endorsement.

**Possible benefit to partners:** reproducible baseline and experimental data; a transparent cost-per-cubic-meter analysis; a practical route to evaluating water resilience at a real site. Whether a prototype is worth scaling remains unknown.

**Commercial boundaries:** Evercraft retains its *preexisting* technology and brand. All rights in *new jointly developed work*, research data, facility access, confidentiality, publication and resulting licenses must be expressly negotiated before collaborative work; university ownership may apply. No exclusivity is assumed.

**Contact:** Reply by email to the Evercraft LLC representative who supplied this brief.
