## Approved-copy candidates (each requires owner approval)

### EVC-PF-01 | Calls
When you're on a roof, under a sink, or halfway through a job, the phone doesn't stop ringing. The customer on the other end doesn't know whether you're busy, understaffed, or simply unable to answer. They just need help.

For a service business, the problem isn't that you don't own another app. It's that an inquiry can disappear between a ringing phone, a text message, a calendar, and the next estimate.

We're exploring better ways for small businesses to capture inquiries, route them to the right person, and keep track of what happens next. Before we talk technology, we'd rather understand the problem properly. If you run a service business, where do new inquiries fall through the cracks most often?

If that sounds familiar, message Evercraft with **CALLS** and tell us how your team handles missed inquiries today.

### EVC-PF-02 | Parts
A repair job can be ready to finish until one component turns out to be impossible to identify. The label is worn down. Three websites disagree about compatibility. The supplier wants a number you don't have.

The real expense isn't just the part. It's time spent searching, repeated callbacks, and the work that stays unfinished while everyone guesses.

We're building FindMyPart around that frustration: getting better information into the search before anyone orders the wrong thing. If you work in repairs, parts, maintenance, or restoration, what's the part you spent far too long trying to identify?

Tell us what equipment it came from. We're looking for specific real-world cases to guide the next demonstrations and pilots.

### EVC-PF-03 | Meetings
The meeting ended. Everyone sounded aligned. Two days later, somebody asks who was supposed to do what.

That isn't a meeting problem. It's a follow-through problem. Decisions are scattered across recordings, chat messages, notebooks, and people's memories.

We're building tools that can help teams turn conversations into clear, reviewable next steps. The important part isn't an AI summary. It's knowing what was decided, who owns the action, and what still needs human confirmation.

For project teams and agencies: which part fails more often, capturing the decision or following through on it? We're speaking with teams that want to test a more reliable workflow.

### EVC-PF-04 | Estimates
A customer asks for a quote. You visit the property, take notes, price the job, send the estimate, and then the whole process slows down.

Maybe the estimate is in an email. The approval happened over text. The invoice lives somewhere else. The payment status needs another login. Small delays become another hour of administrative work when you have jobs to run.

We know how frustrating those handoffs are because we've worked through them in real service operations. We're working to simplify the path from inquiry to estimate, approval, and payment, with clear records along the way.

What is the slowest step in your own process? If you run a contracting or service company, tell us. We'd rather design around the actual bottleneck.

### EVC-PF-05 | Security
Small businesses rarely have a dedicated security department, but they still have customer records, payment workflows, staff accounts, websites, devices, and years of hard-earned trust to protect.

The first question shouldn't be which expensive security package to buy. It should be: what would interrupt your business tomorrow if somebody gained access, changed a setting, or locked you out?

At Evercraft, we're developing practical ways to map those weak points and make protective decisions easier to understand. No scare tactics and no promise that any tool can make an organization risk-free.

If you're running a small business, what system or account would be hardest to lose access to for a day?

### EVC-PF-06 | Systemia
You shouldn't need to open five dashboards just to answer a simple question about your business.

Which customer is waiting? Which project is blocked? Who needs a follow-up? Which invoice has been paid? Which promise did someone make last Tuesday?

The pain isn't a shortage of software. It's that information gets separated from the decision it was supposed to support.

That's the problem we're addressing with Systemia: better operational continuity, clearer ownership, evidence for what changed, and fewer loose ends that depend on one person's memory. We're looking for conversations with operators who have outgrown disconnected workflows.

What question about your business takes far too many clicks to answer?

### EVC-PF-07 | Founders and partners
A founder can spend months building something technically impressive and still struggle to explain why another person should pay for it.

We're learning that lesson in public at Evercraft. The customer doesn't need to admire a software architecture. They need to see a problem they know, understand what might change, and trust that the solution actually works.

So we're changing how we talk about our work. Fewer feature parades. More specific problems, truthful demonstrations, measurable outcomes, and conversations with the people living through those problems.

If you've built a useful product but had trouble getting it in front of the right businesses, which part has been hardest: getting attention, earning trust, or closing the sale?
