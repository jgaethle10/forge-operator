# Go-To-Market Playbook

## The sentence

Evercraft builds evidence-first software that can discover a problem, prove what is happening, execute bounded work, and leave a receipt.

That is the umbrella. Each product still gets its own plain-English promise.

## Distribution order

### 1. Direct buyer doors
Every launch product gets one page that answers:
- What painful moment is this for?
- What do I send you?
- What do I get back?
- What does it cost?
- What will you not do?
- How fast is the first useful result?
- What evidence will I receive?

### 2. Machine doors
Keep the existing CHUM/direct-door/MCP approach, but only advertise live runtime endpoints after external canaries prove them. Publish intent-rich product records so agents can match pain to the narrow product without requiring the buyer to know Evercraft vocabulary.

### 3. Design partners over generic ads
For high-value products, recruit a tiny number of named design partners with a bounded paid study. The deliverable must be useful even if the customer never buys anything else.

### 4. Partner channels
Target businesses already sitting next to the pain:
- ForensiScope: legal-tech vendors, investigators, security integrators, claims firms, media-review teams.
- IBM i Rescue: MSPs, IBM i consultants, ERP/modernization firms, cyber/risk firms.
- Foundry App Escape: agencies, fractional CTOs, AI-builder communities, hosting/migration shops.
- EarthWorks: equipment dealers, fleet telematics vendors, quarries, contractors, autonomy vendors.

The partnership pitch is not "resell our giant platform." It is "use this bounded capability to close or de-risk work you already encounter."

## Commercial architecture

Buyer sees one narrow product.

Behind the curtain:
`Direct Door / CHUM -> Systemia admission -> capability contract -> EverScript mission -> Saban/Yard execution -> Evercraft Payments -> receipt ledger`

This is the moat. Do not make the customer learn the moat before they can buy the shovel.

## Packaging checklist

A product cannot advance to launch until all fields are green:
- buyer
- trigger
- one-sentence promise
- bounded input contract
- bounded output contract
- price or quoting rule
- human authorization point
- payment route
- runtime/fulfillment route
- evidence/receipt format
- support boundary
- safety/legal boundary
- demo/proof case
- human-readable page
- machine-readable discovery
- external canary
- conversion event telemetry

## Sales motion

Use proof-before-pitch.

1. Detect an expensive, specific failure mode.
2. Show enough evidence to prove the pain is real.
3. Offer the smallest paid intervention.
4. Deliver a durable artifact or receipt.
5. Only then expand into implementation, subscription, monitoring, licensing, white-label, or API access.

No mass Gmail loop. No "AI transformation" fog. No dumping a 60-product catalog on a buyer.

## Platform commercialization later

EverScript, Systemia, Saban, Edge, Payments, and Foundry can become standalone infrastructure products, but they first need external proof from the vertical products using them.

The verticals are not distractions from the platform. They are the platform's test pilots, case studies, and distribution engines.
